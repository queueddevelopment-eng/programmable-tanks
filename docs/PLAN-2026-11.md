# Programmable Tanks: plan from here (November 2026)

*Written November 15, 2026 (decisions recorded the same day), after reviewing the project docs against the October work (v0.3 and v0.4 drafts, shot clock). Companion record: `claude/TANKS-V03-V04-NOTES.md` has every October experiment with numbers.*

## Where things stand

The project docs describe the game as of September 22 (v0.2). Since then, in the tank chat, the rules moved to a v0.4 draft that the simulator and the viewer both implement, verified against each other with zero mismatches:

- **Card mechanic:** a fixed chassis (Fire ×2, Hunt, Forward) plus a hand of 5 drawn from one shared deck.
- **Two pit stops** after ticks 10 and 20: draw a card, then play one (insert or replace), or keep the program.
- **Board elements:** pits (2 damage, stuck 2 ticks), Breach (shoot out a wall), Raise wall (behind you).
- **Hunt** never turns to face a wall or pit.
- **New cards:** Otherwise (if/else). Rare one-shot specials in the deck: Shield, Overcharge, Spread shot. Optional pre-placed pickups.
- **Shot clock:** after 10 ticks with no hit, the leader must attack along the shortest route and the other tank holds. Programs resume after the next hit.

Playtesting so far is one person (Q) in the viewer, and the read is that it's more enjoyable.

## Where the October work bends the design constraints

These need your call before anything is frozen.

1. **"Randomness only before programming, never mid-round."** Pit-stop draws happen mid-round. Each card is seen before you reprogram, so it's input randomness at a programming moment, not something that hits a running program. My read is that it fits the spirit, but it's your rule.
2. **"One rule set with no exceptions."** Specials added two rules: a used special is skipped for the rest of the round, and a loop card can't repeat a special. The second is an exception to the card language.
3. **"A loss should mean the code was wrong."** About 18% of hits land while the shot clock is driving. The pathfinding attacker is not something a program can express.
   - **Reconciling option:** define the overtime behaviour as two printed programs in the card language. The attacker runs `If in sights · Fire · Otherwise · Hunt`; the holder runs `If in sights · Fire · Otherwise · Face enemy`. The rule is then "your program is swapped for a known one", not "the computer takes over".
   - **Cost:** greedy Hunt left 4% of rounds stuck, against 0% with pathfinding. Layout curation could close that gap (experiment E2).
4. **"Loser rewrites" is the heart of the game, and dealt hands broke its mechanics.** With a fixed kit, "winner locked, loser rewrites" was simple. With dealt hands nobody has designed it: what the winner keeps, what the loser redraws, and whether a counter is usually reachable from a new hand. This is the most important open design question. Nothing has tested the debug rule under v0.4.

## The biggest risk: can people run this on paper?

Steps 2 and 3 of the staging plan are cards-only, which means people execute the programs by hand. v0.4 leans on a computer in several places:
- Hunt's turn-and-sidestep logic.
- Otherwise remembering the last condition.
- Spent specials and gun cooldowns.
- Two pit stops.
- The shot clock's roles and route.

A strong-vs-strong round now lasts a median of 21 ticks, with two tanks to step through each tick. At 20–30 seconds per tick by hand, that's 7–10 minutes per round, and a match is first to 3 rounds. That may be fine for enthusiasts (Robo Rally runs 5 hand-executed steps per round), but nobody has timed it.

Recommendation: split step 2 into two kinds of session.
- **Decision playtests:** physical cards, with the viewer as referee (enter the programs, watch them run). This tests the design: rewriting, card reach, the shot clock's feel. The laptop is test equipment, not the product, so it doesn't break the phones-away rule.
- **Hand-execution playtests:** tokens, a tick sheet, and a printed quick reference. This tests whether a cards-only edition is viable at all. If it isn't, the cards-only edition needs a simpler execution model, or step 3 changes shape. That's a decision-gate input.

### Phase 2: playtest kit and 5+ sessions (3–5 weeks)

**Kit:**
- Print-and-play PDF of the v0.5 cards, the board, and the layout cards.
- Tokens, the tick sheet, and a one-page quick reference.
- A "referee mode" in the viewer for typing in physical programs fast.
- An observer sheet.

**Record per session:**
- Minutes per round: programming and running separately, by hand vs referee.
- Rules misread, and where.
- Whether the loser *wanted* to rewrite (1–5) and what they changed.
- Cards reached for that didn't exist.
- Whether the shot clock felt fair or felt like the game playing itself.
- Any round that felt stuck.

**Gate to step 3 (all must hold):**
- A hand-run round fits ~10 minutes, or a simpler paper model is identified.
- Losers rewrite willingly in most sessions.
- No rule is misread in more than one session after the quick reference is fixed.
- At least 3 of 5 groups would play again.

### Later (unchanged staging)

Cards-only edition on The Game Crafter → decision gate (crowdfunding, publisher pitch, classroom channel, or stop) → one proof-of-concept tank.

The October hardware notes say what the electronic version will need:
- **Spread shot:** three extra IR emitters (~$1).
- **The new Hunt and the shot-clock attacker:** the tank must know the wall layout. On a passive electronic board that comes free; a self-driving tank would read it from the layout card.
- **Walls:** physical magnet-based blocks placed by hand when the board lights a square.
- **Cost:** BOM research put a self-driving tank at ~$22 at 500 units (~$25–27 landed). The NFC reader plus 20 tag cards is the biggest single cost ($6–8.50).

## Decisions (Q, November 15)

1. **Pit-stop draws are allowed.** A card drawn at a pit stop is seen before you reprogram, so it counts as randomness before programming.
2. **Specials are in the base game, with no exception.** The "loop can't repeat a special" rule is dropped. The spent rule covers it: any statement whose action is spent is skipped, so a loop in front of a special fires it once.
3. **Overtime is written in the card language.** At the shot clock, the attacker runs `If in sights · Fire · Otherwise · Hunt` and the holder runs `If in sights · Fire · Otherwise · Face enemy`. The pathfinding attacker stays in the engine as a comparison only. This makes E2 the critical experiment: printed overtime behaves like greedy Hunt, which stalled about 4% of rounds.
4. **Free-for-all is out of the first edition.** E6 is closed.
5. **The code moves into a repo now.**
